## Hue is an angle

<div class="scene wide" data-scene="pal" data-viewbox="0 0 1600 760"></div>

Notes:
So a colour is an arrow: angle is hue, length is saturation. The Americans got there first, with
NTSC. But any phase error between the studio and your sofa rotates the arrow, and your
newsreader goes green. Hence the hue knob, and "Never The Same Colour". PAL, Walter Bruch at
Telefunken: flip the V component on every other line. Now the same error rotates the next line
the other way. Average the two lines with a delay line and the error cancels. You lose a little
saturation instead of getting the wrong colour.

## Berlin, 25 August 1967

- Funkausstellung, right here in Berlin
- Willy Brandt presses a big red button: colour TV for West Germany
- The button was a dummy
- ...and the colour came on a few seconds _before_ he pressed it

Photo of Brandt at the button (Bundesarchiv / Wikimedia Commons, check licence)

Notes:
And it launched here. The 25th Funkausstellung in West Berlin, 25 August 1967. Willy Brandt,
then foreign minister, presses a big red button to start colour broadcasting. The button wasn't
connected to anything, and the broadcast went to colour a few seconds early, before he'd even
pressed it. Which, for a talk at a programming conference, feels about right.
